Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-10-31

This policy describes what the public relay and browser editor at edit.piconic.ai (“the service”) keeps about you. It is run by kobaken, an individual (kobaken.co); contact kobaken@piconic.ai. The details, kept up to date with the code, are in the public relay notes and the security design.

Your documents

Document updates, comments and pasted images are encrypted on your device before they are sent. The key is in the part of the link after #, which your browser never sends to the service. The service relays encrypted data and cannot read it. A room that pedit opens from existing text files is kept by default: while the host is connected the service holds the host’s latest encrypted snapshot of the files, and when the host leaves the room stays open so that others can keep editing, for a day by default (the host can choose up to 7 days with pedit --keep, or none with pedit --keep 0, a room that stores nothing), counted from the moment the host leaves (a host that comes back gets a new 24 hours of connection, but a room ends at most 7 days after it was opened). While it is kept, the service stores encrypted text, comments and images until the room’s deadline, at most 7 days after the host leaves. A canvas, a file created from a template, a scratch room and a host on Windows get a room that stores none of your text and ends when the host leaves. Your files stay on the host’s computer.

What the service keeps

  • Encrypted images. In a kept room (the default) they are kept until the room’s deadline, whether or not the host is connected. In a room that is not kept (pedit --keep 0) they are kept while the host is connected, and the room deletes them when the host leaves. If a deletion fails, a storage rule expires them after upload (eight days for a kept room, one day for one that is not), and Cloudflare removes them typically within a further day.
  • Kept rooms: an encrypted snapshot of the text and comments, and a log of the encrypted, signed edits and comments made while the host is away. The host’s pedit, run on the file again, takes them in and the service then deletes the log; what is left is deleted at the room’s deadline, at most 7 days after the host leaves. The service sees the size and time of each edit and an opaque identifier of who sent it, never its content.
  • Per room: hashes of the room’s admission tokens, which admit people while the host is away and cannot decrypt anything, and the number and total size of the stored images, deleted when the room ends; and the time the host first connected, kept after the room ends so that the room ID cannot be used again. The room’s storage keeps 30 days of history for point-in-time recovery, which includes a kept room’s encrypted data after it was deleted.

Logs

The service logs requests with Cloudflare Workers Logs, kept for 7 days. A log entry has the time and path of a request and its status. The path contains the room ID; in the entries we looked at, Cloudflare had replaced it with REDACTED, but its rule for that does not catch every room ID. Looking at an entry, it also keeps your IP address, your browser’s user agent, the country, city, postal code, coordinates and time zone Cloudflare derives from the IP address, the network (ASN and organization), and details of the TLS connection such as its version, cipher and client fingerprints. Events in a room, including each relayed message, are logged with their time. The content of the messages is encrypted and is not logged.

Request headers are kept too. This includes the tokens your browser or the pedit command line tool sends to be admitted to a room (in the Sec-WebSocket-Protocol header of the connection, and the X-Pedit-Read-Admission and X-Pedit-Admission headers, which image requests carry too), and headers such as Origin and the browser’s language and client hints. Those tokens cannot decrypt anything, but they let their holder open a connection to that room while the room exists: at most 24 hours while the host is connected and, once the host has left a kept room, until its deadline, at most 7 days later, during which the token admits its holder without the host. The host token, which the command line tool sends in the Authorization header when it opens or closes a room, is masked in the log entries we looked at. This page will be updated if the logging is changed.

Cloudflare

The service runs on Cloudflare, which handles every request and sees your IP address and everything the service sees. See Cloudflare’s privacy policy.

In your browser

The editor’s own code sets no cookies and loads no analytics or tracking scripts. The responses we looked at (the home page and room creation) set no cookie. Cloudflare inserts its Web Analytics script tag into the page; the page’s Content-Security-Policy allows scripts only from the service itself, so a browser should not load it. Cloudflare also adds Network Error Logging headers: if your browser cannot reach the service, it may report the failure to Cloudflare. The editor keeps in your browser’s local storage only the name you typed and display preferences, never a room ID, key or document content. Your browser history keeps the full link, key included.

Third parties

The Markdown preview does not load images or videos from other sites. A link to a video player or to a public file on GitHub loads only after you click it (or choose “Always load” for that provider), and then the provider sees your IP address. The service sees nothing of it. The pedit command line tool itself contacts api.github.com once a day to check for a newer release, with no file name, room or key.

Your requests

The service cannot read your documents, so it cannot show or delete them. To ask what is held about a room, or to ask for a room to be blocked or its data removed, write to kobaken@piconic.ai with the room ID (the 22 characters after /r/, without the # and what follows).
